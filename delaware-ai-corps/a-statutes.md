# Node A: Delaware statutes on AI and corporations

*Note: delcode.delaware.gov and several other primary sites could not be reached from this environment, so the statutory wording below comes from secondary sources and the author's prior knowledge. It has not been checked against the live Code.*

## 1. Can an AI be a director, officer or incorporator?
**Verified (well established):**
- **Directors, §141(b):** "Each director shall be a natural person." An AI cannot sit on the board, and practitioners and SEC filings repeat this rule. The 2014 "Vital" AI that a Hong Kong VC firm put on its board had no vote, and commentators say the same move would not be allowed in Delaware. A charter or bylaws may add qualifications for directors but cannot relax this one. [Eckert Seamans](https://www.eckertseamans.com/legal-updates/artificial-intelligence-and-corporate-governance-issues); [UC Law SF journal (reform proposal)](https://repository.uclawsf.edu/hastings_science_technology_law_journal/vol17/iss1/5)
- **Incorporators, §§101/103/107:** Under §101(a), "any person, partnership, association or corporation" may incorporate. §103 governs who signs filings, and since 2014 an employee or principal of the incorporator may sign. Nothing in these sections treats an AI as a "person." [1967 DGCL text (Widener)](https://delawarelaw.widener.edu/files/resources/dgcl1967.pdf); [MNAT 2014 amendments](https://www.mnat.com/assets/htmldocuments/BylinedArticles/Analysisofthe2014AmendmentstotheDGCL.pdf)
- **Officers, §142:** The bylaws or the board set officers' titles and duties. §142 has no express natural-person requirement. [calcorporatelaw.com](https://www.calcorporatelaw.com/delaware-does-and-doesnt-define-officer)

**Uncertain:** Whether an AI could hold an office or act as an incorporator has not been tested. The prevailing view is that it cannot, because an AI has no legal personhood and cannot sign, consent or owe fiduciary duties. Officers are also agents who owe fiduciary duties (*Gantler v. Stephens*, 2009). No statute or court decision answers the question directly.

## 2. Can a board delegate decisions to AI?
- **§141(a):** The business and affairs of the corporation are "managed by or under the direction of" the board. Delegating to management or tools is allowed, but under common-law limits the board cannot give up its core oversight and decision-making. Commentators say a board can use AI as long as it does not "delegate its essential management functions and rely solely upon AI." [Eckert Seamans](https://www.eckertseamans.com/legal-updates/artificial-intelligence-and-corporate-governance-issues); [Foley playbook (Sept 2026)](https://www.foley.com/es/insights/publications/2026/09/the-board-directors-playbook-responsible-director-use-of-artificial-intelligence/)
- **§141(e):** Directors are "fully protected" when they rely in good faith on records and on reports from officers, employees, committees "or any other person" the director reasonably believes to be within that person's professional or expert competence and who was selected with reasonable care. **Open question:** whether AI output counts as a report from "any other person." Scholars argue it does not clearly fit and that the provision should be rethought. [Richmond JOLT, Jan 2026](https://jolt.richmond.edu/2026/01/21/ai-in-the-c-suite-rethinking-director-reliance-under-dgcl-%C2%A7-141e-in-the-age-of-algorithms)

## 3. Recent statutory developments, 2023–2026
- **SB 21 (signed Mar. 25, 2025):** Rewrote the §144 safe harbor for controller and conflicted transactions and narrowed §220 books-and-records demands. It says nothing about AI. [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/delaware-legislature-codifies-safe-harbors-for-controller-transactions-and-moderates-inspection-demands/); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/04/delaware-changes-its-corporate-law-what-you-should-know-about-senate-bill-21)
- **2026 DGCL amendments (reportedly signed June 10, 2026; effective Aug. 1, 2026):** Targeted changes to §§242, 275 and 312. Search snippets show nothing on AI, and the full text was not checked. [RLF](https://www.rlf.com/2026-amendments-to-the-delaware-general-corporation-law/embed/)
- **HB 16 (2025):** Amends Title 29, not the DGCL. It adjusts the Delaware AI Commission and its AI definitions. Whether it was signed was not confirmed. **HJR 7 (signed July 2025):** Directed the AI Commission and the Secretary of State to design an **agentic-AI regulatory sandbox**, with corporate governance in scope. [Delaware News](https://news.delaware.gov/2025/07/23/delaware-launches-bold-ai-sandbox-initiative/); [AI Commission 2025 report](https://ai.delaware.gov/wp-content/blogs.dir/229/files/sites/229/2025/12/Delaware-Artificial-Intelligence-Commission-Annual-Report-2025.pdf)
- **Proposed "Artificial Intelligence Company" (AIC), July 2026: a draft, not enacted law.** Secretary of State Patibanda-Sanchez and John Nay of Norm Ai unveiled it on about July 13, 2026. Under the draft:
  - An AIC is a new entity whose day-to-day management is handed to an AI agent.
  - It must have one human or corporate member, who keeps limited liability only if the entity is adequately capitalized, logs the agent's activity and tells counterparties it is a test entity.
  - It would run in a 30-month sandbox, with entry approved by a committee that includes the Secretary of State and the Attorney General.
  - The Court of Chancery could dissolve an AIC, and banking is excluded.

  Reports say the draft cleared a subcommittee and is expected to come up in January 2027. Critics (Lipton, Petrin) question its accountability and whether other states would honor its liability shield. [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/esg/ai-run-companies-are-coming-delaware-wants-to-get-ahead-of-them); [Route Fifty, Sept 2026](https://www.route-fifty.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/09/delawares-proposed-ai-company-bill-needs-clearer-accountability-frameworks-experts-say/416210/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/delaware-ai-company-plan-draws-questions-over-who-is-accountable/); [Oxford Business Law Blog, Oct 2026](https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/oblb/blog-post/2026/10/delawares-ai-corporation-and-new-race-bottom)

**Not verified:** the AIC draft's text, its bill number, whether it would sit in the DGCL, the LLC Act or a new chapter, and the exact sponsorship. Sources disagree on whether it was released in June or July.
