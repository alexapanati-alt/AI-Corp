# Node C: Delaware courts, fiduciary duty and AI governance

## 1. Caremark / Marchand and AI-risk oversight
**Verified:** *In re Caremark* (Del. Ch. 1996) and *Marchand v. Barnhill* (Del. 2019) hold that directors must make a good-faith effort to put in place a reasonable reporting system for risks central ("mission critical") to the business. Marchand let a claim proceed where the board had no monitoring of food safety, the central risk for that company. *Boeing* (2021) applied the same reasoning to aviation safety, and *McDonald's* (2023) extended oversight duties to officers.
**Uncertain / commentary only:** No Delaware decision has yet applied Caremark to an AI-risk oversight failure. Commentators say AI risk is likely "mission critical" for AI-centric companies (for frontier labs, almost by definition), and that boards relying on AI compliance tools must check that the reliance is reasonable. All of this is extrapolation.
- https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/03/10/corporate-oversight-in-the-age-of-artificial-intelligence/
- https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/oblb/blog-post/2026/03/algorithmic-oversight-what-directors-and-officers-must-do-comply-caremark
- https://www.dandodiary.com/2026/06/articles/artificial-intelligence/guest-post-ai-governance-is-a-fiduciary-duty/
- https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2022/04/26/ai-oversight-is-becoming-a-board-issue

The likely route for a future case is a books-and-records demand under DGCL §220 followed by a Caremark derivative suit. Chancery's 2019 Facebook data-privacy §220 ruling is the closest analogue. https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/publication-listing/caremark-claims-on-the-rise-fueled-by-section-220-demands

## 2. Litigation over AI-company governance (2023–2026)
- **Musk v. OpenAI was in federal court in California (N.D. Cal., Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers), not Delaware.** In May 2026 an advisory jury found Musk's claims time-barred, and the judge adopted the verdict and dismissed them. https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/may/18/federal-jury-siding-openai-feud-elon-musk/
- **Delaware's role was through the Attorney General, not the courts.** AG Kathy Jennings reviewed OpenAI's recapitalization starting in October 2024, retained an independent financial advisor, secured commitments on safety and mission, and on Oct 28, 2025 did not object to the deal. OpenAI Group PBC is a Delaware PBC controlled by the OpenAI Foundation (the nonprofit). https://news.delaware.gov/2025/10/28/ag-jennings-completes-review-of-openai-recapitalization/
- In April 2026 OpenAI asked the California and Delaware AGs to investigate Musk's conduct. https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/openai-urges-california-delaware-investigate-215410715.html
- **Not found:** I found no Delaware Chancery or Supreme Court opinion (2023–2026) on the internal governance of OpenAI, Anthropic or another AI lab. The Nov 2023 removal and reinstatement of Altman produced no reported Delaware litigation that I could verify. This is a negative search result, not proof that no case exists.

## 3. Delaware PBCs (DGCL §§361–368) and AI labs
**Statute (verified):**
- §362 requires the charter to name a specific public benefit.
- §365(a) requires directors to balance stockholders' pecuniary interests, the interests of those materially affected by the company's conduct, and that public benefit.
- Under §365(c), as amended in 2020, a failure to balance is generally not treated as bad faith or a loyalty breach for exculpation purposes.
- §366 requires periodic benefit reporting to stockholders.
- Under §367, only stockholders holding 2% of shares (for listed companies, the lesser of 2% or $2M in value) may sue to enforce the balancing duty. The public and regulators have no such right, so enforcement is weak.
- https://law.resource.org/pub/us/code/de/title8/c001/sc15/index.html
- https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2020/08/31/delaware-public-benefit-corporations-recent-developments

**Users:**
- Anthropic is a Delaware PBC. Its Long-Term Benefit Trust, a Delaware common-law purpose trust holding Class T stock, elects a growing share of the board. https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-long-term-benefit-trust and https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2023/10/28/anthropic-long-term-benefit-trust/
- OpenAI Group PBC (2025).

**Why AI labs use PBCs (commentary plus my inference):**
- The PBC form gives directors legal cover to put safety and mission ahead of profit maximization, which avoids the *Revlon*/shareholder-primacy pressures of an ordinary corporation.
- It still allows conventional equity fundraising, which a nonprofit cannot.
- Delaware law is familiar to investors.

Critics note that because only stockholders can enforce the balancing duty, the mission commitment is largely self-policed. Nevada, by contrast, reportedly lets its AG enforce a PBC's mission; that claim comes from FLI and I have not verified it. https://futureoflife.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Indicator-Company_Structure__Mandate_v2.pdf
